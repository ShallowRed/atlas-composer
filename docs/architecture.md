# Architecture

Atlas Composer is a Vue application with Pinia stores and a rendering in d3, beside the npm packages of `packages/`. [adrs.md](adrs.md) gives the reasons of the decisions that this document describes.

## Framing

The maintainer set this framing on 2026-11-27, as a working hypothesis to revise when one of its signs appears or at the next architecture audit, including the split into four parts.

The project holds seven architecture characteristics: geometric accuracy, compatibility of the formats, safety of the dependencies, boundaries between modules, maintainability and rendering speed, which are critical, and a bilingual interface, which is kept. Accessibility is a horizon, the weight of the loader is secondary, and privacy is not critical.

The compatibility of the formats goes in two stages. While few projects reuse the packages, a breaking change is allowed and announced in the changelog. Each file carries the version of its format (`version`, `1.x` in `composite-projection.schema.json`), distinct from the npm version of `@atlas-composer/specification`: a change that makes the new schema refuse a valid file, or the old schema accept a file whose meaning changes, moves both to their next major version; an optional addition moves both to their next minor version. A use in production, a project that depends on the format or time spent on compatibility questions moves the project to the stable stage, with a deprecation period.

Each part has its own three priorities:

- The site, the editor and the examples: geometric accuracy, rendering speed and maintainability. The editor shows what the loader draws, which `src/__tests__/roundtrip-loader.spec.ts` checks to 1e-6 pixel; the accuracy concerns the projection, and the drawing may approximate (simplification to the pixel, reused relief image) without any approximation reaching an exported file. The rendering speed has no measure and is judged at acceptance; a slowness noticed there adds one. A shared link or an exported file may stop opening after a change of the format, while few projects use them.
- The format, `@atlas-composer/specification`: compatibility, accuracy (each parameter has a single meaning) and interoperability, since the neutral description of `describeComposite` targets PROJ.
- The libraries that applications load, `@atlas-composer/projection-core` and `@atlas-composer/projection-loader`: accuracy, compatibility and safety of the dependencies. They have no production dependency besides `d3-geo` as a peer of the loader, and adding one is the maintainer's decision. `size-limit` stays an error in `pnpm check`; raising the limit for a justified feature is allowed and written in the changeset.
- The data and the tool, `@atlas-composer/preset-library` and `@atlas-composer/cli`: accuracy, checked by `.github/workflows/gis.yml`, compatibility of the public preset ids and of the options of the CLI, and safety of the dependencies of the CLI.

The application is layered by technical role, as the next section describes. A feature that touches every layer is the sign to revisit this first level, measured at the next audit. The loader is a microkernel: its core validates a file against the schema and composes the sub-projections, and the application that reuses it registers the projection factories. The preparation of the data is a pipeline from `configs/atlases/` and `world-atlas` to the topologies of the site and the geometries of the CLI, and a defect is fixed in the step that produces it.

## Layers

[AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) gives the rules of each layer of `src/`, and `eslint.config.js` checks them. The dependencies go from the interface to the domain. `src/views/` and `src/components/` call the composables and the stores. `src/composables/` calls the stores and `src/services/rendering/`. `src/stores/` calls the services and `src/core/`. `src/services/` calls `src/core/`, and `src/core/` imports none of these layers.

A service that keeps data is an instance, such as `GeoDataService` or `CompositeProjection`. A service of transformations has static methods, such as `TerritoryFilterService`.

## From an action to the map

The change of a territory parameter shows the steps from an action to the rendering. `TerritoryParameterControls` calls `setTerritoryParameter` of the parameters store. The store writes the value in its `ProjectionParameterManager` and increments `territoryParametersVersion`. The manager is a plain class, so these version counters give the reactivity: the getters of the store read the counter, and a `computed` or a watcher that calls a getter runs again after a write.

`MapRenderer` draws each map of the application. `SplitView` and `UnifiedView` use this component for their maps. It calls `useMapWatchers`, which watches the version counter, the view mode, the projections and the display options, and asks for a new rendering. `MapRenderer` groups these requests over 50 milliseconds. During a drag, it renders at each request. A rendering empties the container of the map, asks `useMapRender` for an SVG element, inserts this element and adds the overlays.

`useMapRender` reads the stores and calls `MapRenderCoordinator`, which gives the render options to `Cartographer`. In the `composite-custom` view, `CompositeProjection` reads the entries of the composition and the parameters of each entry, places the sub-projection of each active entry, in the order of the entries and with the bounds of the entry, and builds the composite projection with `buildCompositeProjection` of `@atlas-composer/projection-core`. `D3MapRenderer` writes the path of each territory through the sub-projection of its code, as the `compose` command of `@atlas-composer/cli`, and writes no path for a territory without a sub-projection. Before the drawing, `Cartographer` simplifies each territory to the scale of the projection that draws it. `GeoDataService` weights the points of the topology of the atlas once, with `presimplify` of topojson-simplify and the spherical area of their triangle, and `simplifyTopology` of `src/core/geometry/` keeps in each arc the points whose triangle measures at least `MIN_PIXEL_AREA`, a quarter of a square CSS pixel, at the centroid of each territory that uses this arc. `D3MapRenderer` fills each territory without outline, then draws the borders of the territories of each projection in the group `g.borders`: `mesh` of topojson-client gives each border once, so that a border shared by two territories is drawn once, and `withoutAntimeridianSeams` of `src/core/geometry/` leaves out the segments along the antimeridian where Natural Earth cuts a ring.

A service imports no store, so the geoData store gives `Cartographer` the `entryProvider` object of the composition entries store, the `parameterProvider` object of the parameters store and the `activeTerritoryProvider` object of the view store when it creates the cartographer. `CompositeProjection` calls `getEntries`, `getEffectiveParameters` and `getActiveTerritoryCodes` on these objects at each build and at each export. `getEntries` gives each entry, through `entryName` of `src/core/presets/composition-state.ts`, the name of the atlas configuration for a code that it declares, and the name of the entry otherwise. An atlas without territories in its configuration, such as `world`, has no custom composite, and its cartographer receives none of these objects.

The change of an atlas or of a view mode goes through the actions `selectAtlas` and `selectViewMode` of `src/stores/composition.ts`, which `AtlasConfigSection` calls. `selectAtlas` calls `initializeAtlas`, which clears the parameters, the entries of the composition and the geographic data, applies the default preset of the view mode, loads the default view preset, creates a new `Cartographer`, loads the data and calls `triggerRender`. If this initialization fails, the action selects the previous atlas again and shows an error. `selectViewMode` clears the global `center`, `rotate`, `parallels` and `scale`, clears the entries of the composition when the view leaves `composite-custom`, and loads the default preset of this view, through `initializeAtlas` for the `composite-custom` view.

`triggerRender` increments `renderKey`, which `useMapWatchers` also watches. The actions that replace the state in several stores call it after their last write.

## Stores and composables

A store holds the state of one concept, and [adrs.md](adrs.md) gives the reasons. `src/stores/composition.ts` holds the use cases that replace the state of several stores. The geoData store holds the `Cartographer` of the current atlas, the loaded territories and `renderKey`. The composition entries store, `src/stores/composition-entries.ts`, holds the ordered entries of the custom composite, each with its code, its name and its optional bounds, which only the actions of `src/stores/composition.ts` write: a preset or an imported file gives the entries of the file in its order, and a territory checked or composed in a proposal gets the name and the bounds of the atlas configuration, at its place in the order of this configuration. The app store holds the loading state, from which `MapView` decides to show the skeleton of the map.

A composable holds the logic of the interface. It reads the stores and gives a component computed values, as `useViewState` does for the flags of the view mode, or it handles the mouse events on the map, as `useTerritoryCursor`, `useProjectionPanning` and `useClipExtentEditor` do. The composables that draw, such as `useMapRender`, call `src/services/rendering/`.

## Proposal of a composition

In the `composite-custom` view, `ProposalControls` opens `NewCompositionModal`, where one checks territories of the atlas and chooses an arrangement and a scale. "Compose" calls `startProposal` of `src/stores/composition.ts`. `proposeComposition` of `src/core/territories/proposal.ts` gives the main zone to the territory of largest spherical area and a cell of inset to each other territory, gives each territory the projection of the rule of `suggestProjection` with its reason, and fits it to its zone with `fitTerritory` of `src/core/positioning/fit.ts`, which places a sub-projection with the same functions of `src/core/positioning/placement.ts` as `CompositeProjection`. `applyCompositionState` applies the state and stores the proposal in the preset defaults store, so that the proposal is the reference of "Reset all". A territory checked afterwards in "Territories to include" goes in the first free cell, with `freeCell` and `proposeAddition`, and enters the proposal. A preset, an imported file, or a change of atlas or of view empties the proposal.

## Order of the projection parameters

`ProjectionParameterManager`, in `src/services/parameters/projection-parameter-manager.ts`, holds three levels of parameters. The atlas level comes from the parameters of a `unified` preset. The global level comes from `ProjectionParamsControls`, the panning of the map and the rotation and parallels of a link. The territory level comes from the presets, the imported files, the territory controls and the drag of a territory.

`getEffectiveParameters` merges `{}`, the atlas level, the global level and the territory level, in this order, with `mergeParameters`. Each level replaces the keys of the previous levels that it defines.

`center` and `rotate` describe one position together, so both come from the first level that defines at least one of them, in the order territory, global, atlas. A territory that defines `center` alone therefore has no `rotate`, whatever the global level holds.

For the same reason, `setGlobalParameter` and `setTerritoryParameter` throw an `Error` for these two keys, and `setGlobalParameters` and `setTerritoryParameters` write them together. An update that names one of the two replaces both in its level. [projections.md](projections.md) describes the positioning and the parameter registry.

## State in the URL

`useUrlState` of `src/composables/useUrlState.ts` gives `serializeSharedLink` the state of the stores. `ShareButton` calls `copyShareableUrl`, which builds the link from the current state at this moment and copies it.

The query holds the atlas, the view, the projection, the territory mode, the global `rotate` and `parallels`, and the territory scales and translations. In the `composite-custom` view, these two values are written for each active territory, because the preset of the view gives them. In the other views, the query holds the values that differ from the default values of `parameterRegistry`. `serializeSharedLink`, in `src/core/view/shared-link.ts`, writes the keys that `parseSharedLink` reads.

When `MapView` is mounted, it gives `startMapView` the result of `readSharedLink`. `parseSharedLink`, in `src/core/view/shared-link.ts`, keeps the values of a known form and ignores the others. `startMapView` writes the atlas and the view of the link, and `initializeAtlas` runs once. It applies the default presets of the view, then `applySharedLinkView` writes the projection, the rotation and the parallels of the link before the geographic data loads, and `applySharedLinkTerritories` writes the scale and the position of each territory. They keep a projection that the view proposes, a territory of the atlas and the numbers that `parameterRegistry` accepts.

In the `composite-custom` view, the link of a proposal holds its composed territories (`proposal`), the territories added after it in their order (`added`), its arrangement, its scale and its canvas (`canvas=960x500`), whose sides `parseSharedLink` reads between 1 and `SHARED_CANVAS_MAX_LENGTH` pixels. `initializeAtlas` composes the proposal again once the geographic data is loaded, since the proposal reads the geometries: it applies the canvas of the link, calls `startProposal`, replays each added territory with `addTerritoryToComposite`, then calls `applySharedLinkTerritories`. The link keeps the scale and the position of each active territory. It loses a projection changed by hand, a resized frame and a canvas changed after the proposal, and an unchecked territory comes back checked, as for a preset.

## Application and packages

The application depends on three packages at runtime. It takes the types of the exported file, `validateConfig` and the shared constants from `@atlas-composer/specification`, `CompositeProjection` calls `@atlas-composer/projection-core`, and the examples page draws the presets with `@atlas-composer/projection-loader`, as a reuser of a preset does. `ProjectionLoader` calls the same `buildCompositeProjection`, so the application and the loader share the composition of the projections. The build of `@atlas-composer/projection-loader` includes `@atlas-composer/specification` and `@atlas-composer/projection-core` in its bundle.

The specs of `src/__tests__/` also compare `@atlas-composer/projection-loader` with the application, as [development.md](development.md) describes. `@atlas-composer/preset-library` reads `configs/atlas-registry.json` and the presets that the application also reads, as [presets.md](presets.md) describes.

The application and its tests resolve a package through the `exports` field of its `package.json`, which points to `dist/`. After a change in a package, `pnpm build:packages` gives the application the new code.

## Basemap

`src/core/basemap/sources.ts` declares the basemaps in `BASEMAP_SOURCE_DEFINITIONS`: the elevation of the Terrain Tiles on AWS and the imagery of EOX as raster tiles, and the streets of OpenFreeMap as vector tiles, whose TileJSON gives the template of the tiles. The map shows an `attributionSummary`, when a source has one, with a button that unfolds the full attribution. A `ProjectionFrame` of `src/core/frames/` is a projection and the rectangle of the canvas that it draws; the basemap and the examples both read these frames. The functions of `src/core/basemap/` compute, for each frame, the zoom and the tiles that cover it, without I/O. `renderBasemap` of `src/services/rendering/basemap-renderer.ts` fetches these tiles, the second exception to the `fetch` rule of `eslint.config.js`, and draws one image for the frames of a render. `useMapRender` puts this image under the map with `MapRenderCoordinator.insertBasemap`. `drawRaster` reads the tiles through `sampleTiles` and `tilePositions` of `src/core/basemap/raster.ts`: a bilinear interpolation of the tile pixels, and the inverse of the projection computed on a grid of 8 pixels, with an exact inverse in each cell where the position read on the grid differs from the exact position by more than an eighth of a tile pixel. For a source whose `encoding` is `'terrarium'`, `drawRaster` decodes the PNG of each tile with `fast-png` and draws the tiles that `shadeTile` of `src/core/basemap/relief.ts` computes: the altitude tinted by a linear interpolation between color stops and shaded by the method of Horn, with the pixels of the neighboring tiles at the edges.

Between two renders of the same basemap, `useMapRender` places the previous image on the new map as soon as the map is drawn, and the new image replaces it when it arrives. `provisionalPlacements` of `src/core/frames/` places it frame by frame: `frameMapping` checks that the projection of a frame changed by a scale and a translation only, and a frame whose projection changed otherwise shows no image until the new one.

The deployment renders the relief of each map of the initial view and of the split view of each atlas at the pixel ratios 1, 2 and 3, as [development.md](development.md) describes. `basemapFingerprint` of `src/core/basemap/fingerprint.ts` sums up what the image of a raster basemap depends on: the source, the size of the canvas, the pixel ratio and, frame by frame in the order of the render, the extent and the position on the globe of a grid of points. Before it draws a raster basemap, `useMapRender` asks `cachedBasemap` of `basemap-renderer.ts` for the image of that fingerprint, at the next whole pixel ratio; `cachedBasemap` reads the index `basemaps/<build>/index.json` once per page and returns the image once decoded, or `null`, and the map then draws its basemap. The build, `VITE_BASEMAP_BUILD_ID`, keeps the images of two deployments apart. `MapRenderCoordinator.insertBasemap` gives `data-basemap-fingerprint` to a complete image only: a cached image, or the final pass without a missing tile.

The Content Security Policy of the built page admits the hosts of these tiles, as [development.md](development.md) describes.

## Examples

`ExamplesView` shows the examples that `EXAMPLES` of `src/core/examples/definitions.ts` declares, and the demonstration of the composition. The examples store loads the data: `loadExampleMap` reads the preset of an example and the topology of its entities, which `scripts/prepare-examples.ts` writes to `src/public/examples/`; `loadCompositionDemo` reads a preset, the territories of its atlas and the places of `examples/world-places.json`; `loadEarthquakes` reads the feed of `EARTHQUAKE_FEED` once. `useExampleMap` and `useCompositionDemo` draw these data with `renderExampleMap` and `renderCompositionDemo` of `src/services/rendering/`, through the published loader, and `presetFrames` of `src/services/rendering/preset-frames.ts` gives the frames of a preset to both.
